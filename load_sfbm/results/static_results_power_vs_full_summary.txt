2. WHY DO POWERGRAPHICS AND FULL GRAPHICS SHOW DIFFERENT STRESS VALUES?
----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Commands:
- PowerGraphics:  /GRAPHICS, POWER + /ESHAPE, 1  (ANSYS GUI 3D Section Display)
- Full Graphics:  /GRAPHICS, FULL                (Classical APDL Nodal-Averaged Mode)

1. Root Cause in Structural Modeling (/ESHAPE, 1 & BEAM188):
   In the parametric finite element model, the structural skin (SEC 3), spar webs (SEC 2), and rib webs (SEC 1)
   are modeled using SHELL181 elements, while the spanwise stringers (SEC 4), spar caps (SEC 5), and rib flanges
   (SEC 6..8) are modeled using BEAM188 elements. When /ESHAPE, 1 is active, 1D beam elements are rendered with
   true 3D cross-sectional geometry.

2. Mechanism of Divergence & Component-Level Isolation:
   - In Full Graphics (/GRAPHICS, FULL):
     MAPDL follows classical element-to-node averaging, explicitly ignoring BEAM188 elements:
     "BEAM/PIPE elements are detected in the selected element set. They are ignored in the nodal averaging."
     Furthermore, when all SHELL181 elements (Skin + Spar + Rib) are active simultaneously, nodal averaging at
     shared Spar-Skin junction nodes blends the higher Spar Web stress (16.90 / 20.04 / 24.76 MPa) with the lower
     Skin stress (11.15 / 12.41 / 13.31 MPa), yielding the mixed nodal-averaged values (12.50 / 12.76 / 15.57 MPa).
     Isolating each shell component by Section ID (ESEL, S, SEC, , k -> NSLE, S) reveals the true unblended shell
     stresses: Spar Webs (SEC 2) carry 16.90 to 24.76 MPa, while Composite Skins (SEC 3) carry 11.15 to 13.31 MPa.

   - In PowerGraphics (/GRAPHICS, POWER + /ESHAPE, 1):
     PowerGraphics computes stresses across external geometric facets, including the 3D surface facets of BEAM188
     elements, without cross-component nodal smoothing at T-junctions.
     Component-by-component isolation proves that the global PowerGraphics peak (29.54 / 46.78 / 59.15 MPa) occurs
     in SEC 6 (Interior Rib Flanges, 40 x 1.0 mm BEAM188). As rib count decreases from 14 -> 9 -> 7 (rib bays
     decreasing from 13 -> 8 -> 6), each interior rib flange absorbs a proportionally larger share of the chordwise
     airfoil camber/bending transfer between upper and lower skins.

3. DMX VS. TIP_UZ: COMPREHENSIVE ENGINEERING CLARIFICATION
----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Q: "สรุปเราต้องดู DMX หรือ TIP_UZ ในการรายงานผล?"

1. Definitions:
   - DMX (Displacement Vector Sum Maximum):
     DMX = sqrt(UX^2 + UY^2 + UZ^2) >= 0
     Absolute spatial displacement magnitude of any node in the model.
   - TIP_UZ (Z-Axis Directional Displacement at Wingtip):
     TIP_UZ = UZ at y = SPAN (3978 mm) < 0
     True vertical bending deflection of the tailplane under downward aerodynamic loading.

2. Physical Proportion:
   - Vertical deflection |UZ| accounts for 99.24% of DMX (e.g. In Config 1: 6.716 mm vs 6.768 mm).
   - The minor 0.76% difference (~0.05 mm) is caused by Poisson shortening (UY) and chordwise
     dihedral sweep warping (UX).

3. Recommendation for Project Defense & Thesis:
   - State BOTH values clearly:
     * Use TIP_UZ (-6.72 mm to -7.04 mm) when discussing aircraft flight dynamics, elevator clearance,
       and aeroelastic bending stiffness.
     * Use DMX (6.77 mm to 7.10 mm) when referencing ANSYS GUI plot contour bars.
